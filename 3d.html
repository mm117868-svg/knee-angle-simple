Help
  1. Stand your iPhone or Mac securely to one side. The knee you select should face the camera. Keep one person in the picture.
  2. Allow camera access when asked. On iPhone, open this page in Safari. On Mac, use Safari or Chrome.
  3. Use good light and keep your hip, knee and ankle uncovered and visible. The orange line shows the measured leg; the pale lines show the rest of your skeleton.
  4. Move within a comfortable range. The skeleton is smoothed between frames. The live angle averages the last 0.7 seconds and refreshes up to four times a second, so it will follow movement with a short delay. Stop the camera before moving the device.

What the number means: this is an approximate bend calculated between the thigh and shin in MediaPipe’s estimated 3D world coordinates. The model infers these positions from a single RGB camera; no depth sensor or LiDAR is used. A straight leg is 0°; a right-angle bend is 90°. It cannot distinguish bending from hyperextension or measure clinical extension lag. Camera position, clothing, hidden joints and errors in estimated depth affect the result. The displayed coordinates are model estimates, not measured distances. 3D is not automatically more accurate than 2D.

Prototype: this 3D implementation has not been clinically validated. They are not a diagnosis, recovery score or replacement for a physiotherapist's measurement. Saved readings last only until you reload this page.

Save a reading: hold the position comfortably for a short average. Small movements are okay. Camera images stay in your browser; no video is recorded or uploaded.
